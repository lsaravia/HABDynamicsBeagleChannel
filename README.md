# High-frequency observations of toxigenic microalgae and phycotoxins reveal contrasting HAB dynamics in the Beagle Channel. Harmful Algae

Cadaillon, A. M., Almandoz, G. O., Krock, B., Latorre, M., Flores-Melo, X., Hernando, M., Saravia, L., Maldonado, S., Martin, J., & Schloss, I. R. (2026). High-frequency observations of toxigenic microalgae and phycotoxins reveal contrasting HAB dynamics in the Beagle Channel. Harmful Algae, 159, 103213. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hal.2026.103213

## Abstract

Harmful algal blooms (HABs) pose a recurrent threat to sub-Antarctic coastal
ecosystems such as the Beagle Channel. Here, we used a 15-month high-frequency
dataset (January 2021–April 2022) to assess the spatiotemporal dynamics of toxigenic
microalgae and associated toxins across multiple compartments, including plankton,
bivalves, and passive samplers, together with concurrent oceanographic and
meteorological conditions. A. catenella was the dominant toxic species, producing
paralytic shellfish toxins (PST) during two consecutive austral summers with markedly
contrasting intensity. The 2021 event was moderate and spatially confined, whereas
the 2022 bloom was widespread and severe, with A. catenella abundance and PST
levels in plankton and mussels differing by more than two orders of magnitude
between summers. During the 2022 peak, planktonic PST levels differed by several
orders of magnitude among adjacent stations, while mussels reached similarly high
toxicities, indicating spatial decoupling between instantaneous plankton toxicity and
cumulative shellfish contamination. Additional toxin groups, including DTX1, PTX2,
SPX1, 20-Me-G, and domoic acid, were also detected, reflecting the coexistence of
multiple toxigenic taxa. Exploratory GAMs explained a high proportion of A. catenella
variability and indicated significant associations with water temperature, wind speed,
ciliate abundance, SAM index, and seasonality. These findings show that HAB risk in
complex sub-Antarctic systems cannot be assessed based on a single species, toxin,
or sampling matrix, highlighting the need for high-resolution, multi-method monitoring
frameworks.
